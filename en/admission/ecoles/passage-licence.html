Moving to a license ​

Once you're convinced by the free tier or the full-feature demo, moving to a paid license isn't something you do alone online: it's a step supported by edPage's sales team, who discuss your actual needs with you before setting up the license that fits your school.

How the purchase works ​

  1. You reach out to the edPage team (or they reach out to you, typically during or at the end of your full-feature demo).
  2. Together, you define which modules your school needs and which hosting formula suits you.
  3. The edPage team sets up the license for your school from its internal panel.
  4. Your space immediately reflects the licensed modules — no need to wait for an update or a reinstall.

There's no self-service online payment for buying a license: that's deliberate, so that each license actually matches what your school needs rather than a fixed offer picked alone in a form.

Modules available à la carte ​

ERA is licensed module by module rather than as one block: you choose the ones that match your needs, or the full offer if you'd rather cover everything from the start. The modules cover, among others:

  • school structure and admissions (beyond the public application form already included for free);
  • academic life (classes, timetables, assessments, attendance, boarding, health...);
  • finances (tuition fees, payments, expenses);
  • family relations (parent portal, student portal);
  • the reporting dashboard.

The exact scope of each module and its price are communicated by the edPage team when it's set up — they can evolve and aren't meant to be fixed in this guide.

The two hosting formulas ​

Once the license is purchased, your school chooses between two hosting formulas. The choice isn't trivial: it determines who actually manages your server day to day.

Self-hosted (default) ​

This is the default formula: your school hosts its own installation of ERA, on its own server. The edPage team supports you through the initial setup (transferring the admission data you've already entered, activating the license on your server), but day-to-day hosting — uptime, backups, technical maintenance — is then up to your school or its technical provider.

This formula suits schools that already have (or want) control over their own infrastructure.

Managed (paid) ​

With this formula, edPage keeps hosting your school on its infrastructure — exactly as during your free signup and demo, except that read-write access stays open continuously, with no time limit and no read-only freeze. This hosting service is billed on top of the license itself.

This formula suits schools that would rather not manage a server at all and leave that responsibility to edPage.
